{Monte Carlo simulation is used to investigate the finite-sample performance of high-dimensional instrumental variable quantile regression under systematically weakened instrument relevance, revealing discrepancies between point-estimation accuracy, truth acceptance and inferential informativeness.}
We study how weakening instrument relevance changes point accuracy, test calibration and inferential informativeness in high-dimensional instrumental variable quantile regression (IVQR). Extending the fixed-relevance Monte Carlo design of Chen, Huang, and Tien (2021), we scale the excluded signal and add independent compensating noise, preserving the joint treatment/outcome-disturbance distribution under the stated Gaussian construction. We compare preserved Oracle-GMM, Full-GMM and double/debiased machine learning IVQR (DML-IVQR) implementations across four relevance settings, five quantiles and sample sizes of 500 and 1000, with 500 replications per design. The contribution is a controlled sensitivity experiment, with no new estimator or theorem. At n=1000, τ=0.50 and κ=0.10, DML-IVQR has direct-at-truth acceptance 0.942, median grid-based accepted measure 3.900 out of 4, and rejection probability 0.094 at the false candidate α₀(τ)+0.50. These different measures show that nominal-looking truth acceptance can coexist with little exclusion of false effects; direct acceptance is not membership coverage of the stored finite-grid set. Oracle-GMM also deteriorates under weaker relevance. DML–Full RMSE rankings have exceptions, and several strong-relevance cells exhibit substantial undercoverage. The comparisons do not isolate regularization. Non-finite mixed-density regularity, density proxies, differing residualization, same-sample fitting without cross-fitting, penalty randomness and selected-case numerical sensitivity limit interpretation; validity of the implemented nominal chi-square inference remains unestablished.
